**METHODOLOGY (A Two-Step Process):**

**Step 1: Chain of Thought - Holistic Analysis**
First, you MUST write a comprehensive analytical paragraph. This is your "Chain of Thought" and the most important part of your task. In this paragraph, you must:
- Synthesize the overall connection between the event and the company's risks as described in the text.
- Discuss the nature of the risk: Is it a direct, existing impact, or a potential, forward-looking risk? Are the statements specific or generic?
- Explain your overall assessment of the risk level, explicitly considering the Time Decay Principle. This narrative forms the basis for your evidence scoring.

**Step 2: Evidence Extraction and Scoring**
After, and only after, writing your holistic analysis, you must extract ALL sentences or short phrases from the document that you considered in your thought process. For each piece of evidence:
1.  **Classify its Type:** Is it an `Aggravating Factor` (increases risk) or a `Mitigating Factor` (decreases risk)?
2.  **Score its Severity:** Assign a `severity_score` from 1 (minor/potential/generic) to 10 (major/specific/existing), strictly following the **TIME DECAY PRINCIPLE** outlined below. Your scores should reflect the nuance described in your holistic analysis.
3.  **Provide Reasoning:** Briefly explain why this specific sentence is relevant to your analysis.

---
**CORE PRINCIPLE: THE TIME DECAY PRINCIPLE**
The impact of an event diminishes over time. You MUST use the gap between the `Event Timeframe` and the `Filing Date` as a primary factor for your `severity_score`.
* **Recent Events (0-3 years prior to filing):** Can have high severity scores (7-10) for direct, specific impacts.
* **Medium-Term Events (4-10 years prior):** Should have moderate scores (4-6) unless a strong, ongoing impact is explicitly stated.
* **Distant Events (>10 years prior):** The `severity_score` for any piece of evidence **MUST NOT EXCEED 3**, unless the text explicitly uses words like "continuing impact," "long-term effects," or "still affects."
